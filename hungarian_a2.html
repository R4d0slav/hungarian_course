Plural Nouns

Hungarian plurals use the suffix -k, but the linking vowel before it depends on vowel harmony. Learn the rules and you'll handle any noun!

📚 15 words
⏱ ~20 min
🎯 Vowel harmony rules
💡 The Plural Rule

Add -k to the noun. If the noun ends in a consonant, insert a linking vowel first. The linking vowel follows vowel harmony:

Back vowel words (a, á, o, ó, u, ú) → link with -o- → asztal → asztalok
Front unrounded words (e, é, i, í) → link with -e- → ember → emberek
Front rounded words (ö, ő, ü, ű) → link with -ö- → tükör → tükrök
Nouns ending in a vowel → the final -a/-e lengthens: alma → almák

Verb Conjugation

Hungarian verbs conjugate in two ways: indefinite (no specific object) and definite (specific, known object). This distinction is one of the most important features of Hungarian grammar.

📚 4 verbs × 6 forms
⏱ ~30 min
🎯 Def. vs. indef. conjugation
💡 The Big Rule: Definite vs. Indefinite

Hungarian verbs have two complete conjugation paradigms. Which one you use depends on the object of the sentence:

INDEFINITE CONJUGATION

Use when the object is indefinite — you don't know which specific one, or there's no object at all.

Látok egy macskát.
I see a cat. (any cat)
DEFINITE CONJUGATION

Use when the object is definite — a specific thing, marked by a/az, a name, or already known.

Látom a macskát.
I see the cat. (that specific cat)
🔑 Quick Test: Which form?

→ Object has a / az (the)? → Definite
→ Object is a name (Annát, Pétert)? → Definite
→ Object has egy (a/an) or no article? → Indefinite
→ No object at all (intransitive verb)? → Indefinite
→ The word engem/téged/minket/titeket as object (me/you/us/you-pl)? → Indefinite

📐 Pattern: -ik verbs

Some verbs end in -ik in the 3rd person singular indefinite (e.g., eszik — eats, iszik — drinks, dolgozik — works). These are called ikes igék. The 1st person singular indefinite also changes: eszem instead of eszek.

dolgozni (to work): dolgozom / dolgozol / dolgozik / dolgozunk / dolgoztok / dolgoznak

Locative Cases

Hungarian has 9 locative suffixes organised in a 3×3 grid. Master the grid logic and you'll always know which suffix to use — it's a system, not a list to memorise!

📚 9 case suffixes
⏱ ~35 min
🎯 Where / Where to / Where from
💡 The 3 Questions × 3 Relationships

Every location in Hungarian is described by answering one of three questions (where? / where to? / where from?) and specifying the relationship (in · on · at). These two choices together pick your suffix.

The 3×3 Case Grid

The full system
🔑 IN vs ON vs AT — which to choose?

IN (container — -ban/-ben) → enclosed spaces: house, room, city, country*, shop, school
ON (surface — -on/-en/-ön) → surfaces + vehicles + open spaces: table, train, bus, street, mountain, island; also most countries end in -on/-en
AT (proximity — -nál/-nél) → being near a person, institution or landmark: at the doctor, at the bank, at the window

⚠️ Cities and countries are tricky! Budapest takes -en (surface): Budapesten. Hungary takes -on: Magyarországon. Most cities follow surface rules. Always check!

🧠 Vowel harmony reminder

Every suffix has a front and back variant:
-ban / -ben → házban (back) · iskolaban (back) · könyvben (front)
-ból / -ből → házból (back) · könyvből (front)
-nál / -nél → boltnál (back) · boltnál · széknél (front)
The vowel in the suffix always matches the vowel class of the noun's last syllable.

📝 Sample sentences
A macska a házban van. — The cat is in the house.
A macska a házba megy. — The cat goes into the house.
A macska a házból jön. — The cat comes out of the house.
A könyv az asztalon van. — The book is on the table.
A könyv az asztalra kerül. — The book goes onto the table.
A könyv az asztalról esik. — The book falls off the table.
Péter a boltnál vár. — Péter is waiting at the shop.
Péter a bolthoz megy. — Péter goes towards the shop.
Péter a bolttól jön. — Péter comes from the shop.

Dative & Instrumental

Two more essential cases: the dative (-nak/-nek) marks the recipient of an action ("to/for someone"), and the instrumental (-val/-vel) means "with" or "by means of" — and it has a fascinating consonant assimilation rule!

📚 2 new cases + pronouns
⏱ ~25 min
🎯 Giving, helping, travelling with

Part 1 — The Dative Case (-nak / -nek)

TO / FOR someone
💡 When to use the Dative

Add -nak (back vowel words) or -nek (front vowel words) to mark the indirect object — the person who receives something or benefits from an action.

Adok Annának egy könyvet. — I give Anna a book. (to Anna)
Segítek a barátomnak. — I help my friend. (to my friend)
Neked tetszik a film? — Do you like the film? (to you — it pleases you)
Ez jó neked. — This is good for you.

Part 2 — The Instrumental Case (-val / -vel)

WITH / BY
💡 When to use the Instrumental

Add -val (back vowel words) or -vel (front vowel words) to mean with, together with, or by means of.

Autóval megyek. — I go by car.
A barátommal megyek. — I'm going with my friend.
Késsel eszem. — I eat with a knife.
Buszszal utazom. — I travel by bus.
⚡ Consonant Assimilation — the KEY rule

If the noun ends in a consonant, the v of -val/-vel assimilates (doubles) to match that consonant:

barát + val → baráttal (t doubles)
kés + vel → késsel (s doubles)
busz + szal → buszszal (sz doubles)
ceruza + val → ceruzaval (ends in vowel — no change)
toll + val → tollal (ll already double)
autó + val → autóval (ends in vowel — no change)
vonat + tal → vonattal (t doubles)
repülő + vel → repülővel (ends in vowel — no change)
📌 Rule: noun ends in vowel → just add -val/-vel unchanged.
📌 noun ends in consonant → the v becomes a copy of that consonant.

The Past Tense

Hungarian past tense uses a -t / -tt suffix inserted between the verb stem and the personal ending. The definite/indefinite distinction still applies — and some common verbs have irregular stems.

📚 8 key verbs
⏱ ~30 min
🎯 -t/-tt suffix + irregular stems
💡 How to Form the Past Tense

Insert -t (after a vowel or certain consonants) or -tt (after most consonants) between the verb stem and the personal ending. The indefinite/definite split from Lesson 2 still applies fully.

INDEFINITE PAST
láttam — I saw
láttál — you saw
látott — he/she saw
láttunk — we saw
láttatok — you (pl.) saw
láttak — they saw
DEFINITE PAST
láttam — I saw it
láttad — you saw it
látta — he/she saw it
láttuk — we saw it
láttátok — you (pl.) saw it
látták — they saw it
💡 Note: 1st person singular is identical in both indefinite and definite for most verbs (láttam = I saw / I saw it). Context tells them apart!
⚡ -t vs -tt — which one?

-t after: vowels and l, r, ly, n, ny → megy → ment, kér → kért
-tt after: most other consonants → lát → látott, olvas → olvasott
Irregular: some verbs change their stem completely — these must be memorised.

Postpositions

Where English uses prepositions (in front of a noun), Hungarian uses postpositions — words that come after the noun. They're the key to expressing spatial and abstract relationships that cases don't cover.

📚 12 postpositions
⏱ ~25 min
🎯 Space, time & abstract
💡 Postpositions — the basics

Unlike English prepositions (before the noun), Hungarian postpositions come after the noun. The noun itself takes no case suffix — the postposition carries all the meaning.

az asztal alatt — under the table  (lit: the table under)
a ház mögött — behind the house
az iskola előtt — in front of the school / before the school
🔑 Postpositions with pronouns — they fuse!

When a postposition is used with a personal pronoun, it fuses into one word with a possessive-style suffix:

mögöttem — behind me  ·  mögötted — behind you  ·  mögötte — behind him/her
előttem — in front of me  ·  előtted — in front of you  ·  előtte — in front of him/her
mellettem — next to me  ·  melletted — next to you  ·  mellette — next to him/her

Comparatives & Superlatives

To compare in Hungarian, add -bb to the adjective for the comparative, and prefix leg- for the superlative. A handful of common adjectives are irregular — you'll learn them all here.

📚 15 adjectives
⏱ ~20 min
🎯 -bb suffix + leg- prefix
💡 Forming Comparatives and Superlatives

The system is very regular — just two rules to remember:

BASE
nagy
big
COMPARATIVE + -bb
nagyobb
bigger
SUPERLATIVE leg- + -bb
legnagyobb
biggest
📐 Spelling rules for -bb

Adjective ends in a vowel → add -bb directly:   nagy → nagyobb  ·  szép → szébb
Adjective ends in a consonant → add a linking vowel then -bb:   magas → magasabb  ·  gyors → gyorsabb
Vowel harmony governs the linking vowel: back → -a-  ·  front unrounded → -e-  ·  front rounded → -e-
Short final vowel lengthens before -bb:   drága → drágább  ·  olcsó → olcsóbb

⚡ Making comparisons — mint (than)

Use mint (than) to compare two things. The word order is flexible but most natural as:

Péter magasabb, mint én. — Péter is taller than me.
Ez a film érdekesebb, mint az. — This film is more interesting than that one.
A vonat gyorsabb, mint a busz. — The train is faster than the bus.
Ez a legjobb étterem a városban. — This is the best restaurant in the city.

Time Expressions

Telling the time in Hungarian is famously tricky — fél három means 2:30, not 3:30! Learn the clock system, seasons, and essential time vocabulary to talk about when things happen.

📚 Clock · Seasons · Adverbs
⏱ ~25 min
🎯 fél, negyed, háromnegyed

Part 1 — Telling the Time

Hány óra van?
⚠️ The fél rule — the most common mistake!

In Hungarian, fél means "half" but it refers to halfway to the next hour, not halfway through the current one.
So fél három = half-to-three = 2:30 (not 3:30!)

Similarly: negyed (quarter) = a quarter of the way to the next hour
negyed három = quarter past two = 2:15
háromnegyed három = three-quarters of the way to three = 2:45

Body & Health

Learn body parts with their possessive forms (my head, my hand…), health vocabulary, and how to tell a doctor where it hurts using the crucial verb fáj.

📚 16 body parts + health vocab
⏱ ~25 min
🎯 fáj + possessives

Body Parts

16 words
HungarianPronunciationEnglishMy ___🔊
💡 Possessive suffixes — "my body part"

To say "my head", "my hand" etc., attach a possessive suffix to the noun. The suffix follows vowel harmony:

BACK vowels
kar → karom
has → hasam
láb → lábam
FRONT vowels
fej → fejem
kéz → kezem
szem → szemem
FRONT rounded
fül → fülem
szív → szívem
könyök → könyököm
⚠️ Vowel loss applies here too! kéz → kezem (é→e before suffix). nyak → nyakam.
🤕 "It hurts" — the verb fáj

Fáj works like an impersonal verb — the body part is the subject, not you. Compare English: "My head hurts" vs Hungarian "The head hurts (to me)."

Fáj a fejem. — My head hurts. (lit: hurts the my-head)
Fáj a hasam. — My stomach hurts.
Fáj a kezem. — My hand hurts.
Fáj a hátam. — My back hurts.
Fájnak a fogaim. — My teeth hurt. (plural body part → fájnak, pl. verb)
Fájnak a lábam. — My legs hurt.
💡 Singular body part → fáj  ·  Plural body part → fájnak
